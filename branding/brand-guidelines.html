Hush

A creative person's life saver.

Why Hush exists

Your Mac shouldn't sound like it's taking off.

Somewhere in the background, a badly-behaved Adobe helper process is quietly redlining a CPU core — spinning fans, draining battery, heating a lap — while the person at the keyboard has no idea why, and no interest in finding out.

Hush isn't a system monitor. It doesn't teach anyone what a process is, and it never will. It finds the one thing making noise in the background and turns it down, automatically, so the work stays quiet.

Every choice in this identity should protect that feeling: competence without technicality. A hand quietly closing a door, not a dashboard of dials.

Logo & mark

The wordmark is the logo.

Hush has no separate pictorial icon in running text or on the website — the wordmark, set in the display serif at generous size, carries the whole identity. Lowercase in body contexts, sentence case as a name (Hush), never all-caps, never a logotype lockup with a tagline glued underneath.

App icon & menu bar glyph

The menu bar glyph is the equalizer motif from the cover, reduced to five bars settling to one height — the same visual idea as the wordform, at utility scale. It should read instantly as "settling down," not as an audio app.

Minimum clear space: the full height of the wordmark's cap-height on all sides. Minimum digital size: 16px (menu bar), below which use the flat single-bar reduction.

Do Hush

Set in the display serif, generous letterspacing at large sizes, plenty of surrounding quiet.

Don't HUSH

Never bold sans caps — it reads as an alert, the opposite of the brand.

Color

Dark plum, kept quiet.

One deep plum family, used the way a good tailor uses black — as depth, not decoration. The palette stays almost monochrome on purpose: no traffic-light red/green anywhere in the product, because Hush never wants to look alarmed.

Voice & tone

Explain the feeling, never the mechanism.

The person using Hush does not know what a CPU is and does not want to learn. Every sentence should sound like a considerate housemate, not a systems engineer.

01

No jargon, ever. Banned words: CPU, process, throttle, thread, utilization, kill. Say "background task," "quieted down," "working overtime."

02

Calm, not alarmed. Nothing is ever "critical" or "detected." A fan spinning up is described the way you'd reassure a friend, not flag an incident.

03

Short and warm. One idea per sentence. Prefer a period to an exclamation point — confidence doesn't need volume.

Avoid Detected high CPU utilization (94%) from AdobeCrashProcessor.app. Throttling process to 20% priority.
Use Your Mac was working overtime in the background. We've quieted it down.
Avoid 3 processes throttled. CPU load reduced from 380% to 60%.
Use Hushed 3 loud processes today. Your fans should thank you.

In the product

The menu bar is the whole interface.

Most people will only ever see Hush as a single glyph in the menu bar and the small dropdown beneath it. That surface carries the entire brand — it has to feel finished at 16px, not just at poster size.
